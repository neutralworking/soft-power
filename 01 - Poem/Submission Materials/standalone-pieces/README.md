# Standalone pieces — magazine submission strategy

Three pieces lifted from *Soft Power* to submit to literary magazines
ahead of (and alongside) the agent campaign. A single acceptance is
worth more to the agent submissions than three more cold queries.

## Suggested targets

Submit simultaneously where the magazine allows it (most do; declare it).
Each piece can go to two magazines at once.

### Mind Rain

1. **The London Magazine** — long poems welcome, fast turnaround, prestige.
   **Keep the italic context note** (*“Norah Healy, on a Eurostar from
   Paris.”*) — fits their dramatic-monologue convention.
2. **The White Review** — formal experiment, breakup material fits.
   **Strip the context note.** They lean abstract / international; let
   the poem fend for itself, open cold on *“Forever bound to you…”*

### I Feel No Guilt

1. **PN Review** (Carcanet) — high prestige, exactly the territory.
   **Keep the context note.**
2. **Magma** — voice-driven, dramatic-monologue-friendly. **Keep the
   context note** — Magma readers expect character framing.

### Beggar’s News

1. **Bath Magg** — short, sharp, contemporary. **No note needed**
   (none in the file).
2. **Poetry London** — topical / political readiness. **No note needed.**

If any of these aren’t currently open for submissions, fall back to:
*The Rialto, The North, Poetry Review, The Manchester Review,
ANMLY (US), Granta Poetry.* Same context-note logic: keep for
traditional / dramatic-monologue-leaning magazines, strip for
formally abstract ones.

## Adapting inline when you send

You’ll be adapting per magazine in Submittable or email. Keep this open
in another tab. For each piece, paste the verse and then either:

- include the italic line at the top (London Mag, PN Review, Magma); or
- strip it and let the poem open on its first verse line (White
  Review, Bath Magg, Poetry London).

---

## Simultaneous submission etiquette

- Most magazines accept simultaneous submissions; **declare it in the
  cover note** (one sentence: *“This piece is submitted simultaneously
  elsewhere; I will withdraw immediately if accepted.”*).
- A few don’t — check each magazine’s guidelines. If they don’t accept
  simultaneous, send to them alone for the duration of their reading
  window, then move on if no response.
- If a piece is accepted, **email the other magazines immediately** to
  withdraw it. Failure to do so burns bridges.

## Cover note template (for magazine submissions)

Magazines want short, plain, informative. Not a query letter — a note.

> Dear Editor,
>
> Please find attached *[title]*, a [40-line / long / short] poem.
> The piece is lifted from a verse drama in progress, *Soft Power*,
> set across three Easter days in the Camden music scene; it stands
> on its own as a [dramatic monologue / verse-letter / rant].
>
> This piece is submitted simultaneously elsewhere; I will withdraw
> immediately if accepted.
>
> Brief bio: Luke Warrington was born and raised in Camden and went
> to Highgate, where Eliot taught Betjeman in 1916. *Soft Power* was
> first staged at The Nines, Brixton, in 2017. He lives in Prague.
>
> Yours,
> Luke Warrington
> luke.warrington@gmail.com

Adjust the descriptor (“dramatic monologue” / “verse-letter” / “rant”)
to fit each piece.

## A note on Submittable

Most UK and US magazines now use **Submittable** for poetry intake.
Create one account, save the cover note as a template, then attach the
relevant piece per submission. This will take about an hour for the
first six sends. Subsequent submissions are five minutes each.

---

## Track responses

A simple table in `submission-tracker.md` is enough. Date sent, magazine,
piece, response (or silence at week 12). Most reading windows are
2–4 months — set expectations accordingly.
