Dear Emma,

In Camden everyone is almost famous. *Soft Power* is a dramatic poem in
three acts and ten scenes, moving across the three Easter days of Maundy
Thursday, Good Friday and Holy Saturday in the Camden music scene. The
first line is also, by design, the last: the book is a loop, and the cast
the final scene appears to kill is the same cast the first scene mourns.

The story belongs to Jolyon Dox, a record-label A&R man at his younger
brother Cosmo’s wake in a Camden basement called Sunset. By Holy Saturday
he has discovered Cosmo never died at all, the funeral was theatre, and
the gig the new band are about to play at Sunset will be the cast’s last
visible night. The form sits in the lineage of Dante and Eliot; the comps
I’d point to are **Max Porter, *Grief is the Thing with Feathers***
(Faber, 2015), **Kae Tempest, *Brand New Ancients*** (Picador, 2013) and
**Inua Ellams, *The Half-God of Rainfall*** (4th Estate, 2019) — verse-
drama in book form, performable, mythic, with London on every page. The
manuscript is complete in V2 draft, roughly 80 pages typeset.

I was born and raised in Camden and studied at Highgate School, where
T.S. Eliot taught John Betjeman in 1916 — the lineage the book opens on,
taking its epigraph from Betjeman. In my teens and twenties I DJed and
wrote songs on the London indie circuit. *Soft Power* was first staged
at The Nines, Brixton, in 2017, with Slix Fleeingham of Ruff Sqwad
playing Joly. I live in Prague.

I am submitting this to a small number of agents simultaneously. Attached:
a typeset sample of the opening pages and the closing scene (twenty pages,
demonstrating how the book sits on the page) and a short synopsis. The
full manuscript is ready and will follow on request.
