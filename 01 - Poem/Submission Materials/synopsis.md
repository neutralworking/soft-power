# Soft Power — Synopsis

Two versions: a one-paragraph synopsis for queries, and a one-page synopsis
for proposals or when an editor asks for "a bit more." Both withhold the
mechanics of Cosmo's deception and the precise nature of the final-scene
"deaths" — those are book-experience details. The loop is named explicitly.


## One paragraph (≈ 140 words — for queries)

*Soft Power* follows Jolyon Dox, a London record-label A&R man, across the
three Easter days of Maundy Thursday, Good Friday and Holy Saturday. Act One
opens at Cosmo’s wake — Joly’s younger brother, drowned off the Italian
coast — at a Camden basement called Sunset; ten scenes later the cast will
assemble at Sunset again for a gig they will not survive in any conventional
sense. Between those bookends, Joly walks the canal at dawn; Norah Healy
writes a breakup poem on a Eurostar from Paris; Ron Dox, Joly’s father,
delivers a monologue from his collapsing record label; a masked rapper
performs at a Camden pub called The Albion. The first line of the book is
also its last. The cast the final scene appears to kill is the same cast it
conjures back to begin again.


## One page (≈ 400 words — for proposals)

**Soft Power**
A dramatic poem in three acts and ten scenes. Approximately 80 pages typeset.

Jolyon “Joly” Dox is an A&R man at his father’s failing London record label.
His younger brother Cosmo — the family’s prodigy, the runaway, the one who
craved fame — has just been declared dead after a body that was never found
in the Port of Spezia. *Soft Power* opens at Cosmo’s wake in a Camden
basement called Sunset and follows Joly across the three Easter days of
Maundy Thursday, Good Friday and Holy Saturday.

**Act One** (Maundy Thursday). Joly drinks his way through Cosmo’s wake,
reading his brother’s parting letter while a friend pulls him into the
toilets to do cocaine. On the Eurostar from Paris, Norah Healy — Cosmo’s
last great attachment — composes a breakup verse for the man she has just
left behind. At Sunset, a masked rapper appears who calls himself
Rückenfigur, scattering the mourners with a political tirade Joly recognises
in the voice. In a friend’s kitchen, the rapper WK Blue and the poet-
programmer Kwame Kofi Kinsey read each other lyrics until the lights cut and
WK is gone.

**Act Two** (Good Friday). Joly walks the Camden canal at dawn and meets a
beggar with a newspaper. His father, Baron Ron Dox, delivers a monologue
(“I feel no guilt”) from his collapsing office. Norah walks Archway Road
with her friend Evangeline, whose stage career has just ended; they reach
a flat called Captain Never’s, which the new owners are clearing out. By
evening they are walking to a pub called The Albion.

**Act Three** (Holy Saturday). At The Albion, WK and Rückenfigur trade
verses while the publican Bootsy recites a limerick about a dead friend.
Joly arrives and the masked rapper is revealed: Cosmo, not dead, missing
his front teeth. In a bedsit upstairs, Norah reads aloud the eulogy she
wrote for Cosmo’s funeral. The cast converge at Sunset for the opening gig
of a new band’s tour. The performance ends in petrol, vodka, and *“Shantih.”*

The first line of the book is also its last. The cast the final scene
appears to kill is the same cast it conjures back to begin again.
